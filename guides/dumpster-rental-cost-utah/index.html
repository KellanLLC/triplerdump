Where overweight charges actually come from

In our experience the overage is almost always heavy material, not a bin that was too full. Older homes around Ogden are the usual example: plaster and lath walls, two or three layers of roofing, brick. That debris is dense, so a bin can reach its weight allowance before it looks full.

If your job is plaster, tile with mortar beds, roofing, or anything with concrete, dirt, brick or rock in it, call us at (801) 564-3164 before you book. We will tell you how much can safely go in and which size makes sense. It is cheaper to plan for weight than to pay for it afterward.

Is it cheaper to haul it to the dump yourself?

Sometimes, yes. If the whole job fits in one or two pickup loads and you already have a truck, haul it yourself and keep the money. Here is what the local dumps charge so you can do the math.

Weber County Transfer Station (867 W Wilson Lane, Ogden) charges $35.50 per ton for general waste, with a minimum fee of $17 for qualifying Waste Management customers and $27 for everyone else, as of July 1, 2026. The county rate sheet notes that those unit rates do not include taxes, an energy surcharge or other fees, and that uncovered commercial loads are charged double. Weber County rate sheet

Davis Landfill in Layton, run by Wasatch Integrated, charges Davis County district residents a flat $10 per load for loads at or below 10,000 pounds, and out-of-district customers $50 per load, as of September 2, 2026. Bring proof of residency for the lower rate. Wasatch Integrated fee schedule

So the dump fee itself is not the expensive part, especially in Davis County. What a rental saves you is the truck, the trips and the time. A 20 yard bin holds about seven pickup loads. Hauling that yourself means seven rounds of loading, tying down and covering the load, driving, waiting at the scale and unloading, plus a gate fee every trip.

Covering the load is not optional. Wasatch Integrated charges $10 at the gate for each uncovered or unsecured load, and its fee page quotes the Utah law that makes driving with uncovered trash a fine of not less than $200.

Our rough rule: one or two loads, haul it yourself. Three or more, or a job you will be filling over several days, rent the bin.

Other ways to spend less

  • Pick the right size the first time. Going up one size usually costs less than a second bin.
  • Bin switch for long jobs. If one bin fills before the job is done, we swap the full one for an empty one in the same trip for ${{TRD:binswitch}}, and the new bin is billed as a normal rental.
  • Junk removal if you do not want to load anything. ${{TRD:junk}} flat, weekends only: our crew loads, hauls and pays the dump for one load. See junk removal.
  • Dump trailer if you want to haul it yourself. ${{TRD:trailer_day}} per day plus a ${{TRD:trailer_dep}} refundable deposit. You tow it, load it and pay the dump fee yourself. See dump trailer rental.

How much does a dumpster rental cost for a week?

How much is a dumpster rental for a day?

We do not have a separate one-day price. The same price covers anywhere from one day to seven, so if you finish early, call and we will pick it up sooner.

Is the dump fee included in the price?

Yes. Delivery, pickup and the dump fee are included, up to {{TRD:tons15}} tons in a 15 yard, {{TRD:tons20}} tons in a 20 yard and {{TRD:tons25}} tons in a 25 yard. Weight over that is ${{TRD:over_ton}} per ton.

Is it cheaper to rent a dumpster or go to the dump?

For one or two pickup loads, hauling it yourself is usually cheaper if you already have a truck; local gate fees start at about $10 to $27 a load for residents (Davis Landfill charges out-of-district customers $50 a load). For three or more loads, a bin usually wins once you count the trips, the time and a gate fee on every run.

We dump it, the scale ticket shows the weight, and anything over the allowance for your size is billed at ${{TRD:over_ton}} per ton to the card on file. Call before you load heavy material and we will help you avoid it.
