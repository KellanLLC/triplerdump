What can and can't go in a dumpster?

What can and cannot go in a roll-off dumpster in Utah, and where tires, fridges, mattresses, paint and electronics go instead in Weber and Davis County.

Household junk, furniture, remodel and construction debris, roofing and yard waste can all go in a roll-off dumpster. Hazardous waste, liquids such as wet paint, asbestos, propane tanks, fuel, tires, appliances with refrigerant, batteries and electronics cannot. Mattresses need a call first, and so do concrete, dirt, brick and rock because of their weight.

What goes in

  • Household junk and furniture: couches, chairs, tables, dressers, boxes, toys, clothing.
  • Remodel and construction debris: drywall, lumber, cabinets, doors, windows, flooring, carpet and pad, trim.
  • Roofing: shingles, underlayment and roof boards. They are heavy, so see the dumpster size guide first.
  • Yard waste: branches, brush, bushes, old landscaping.
  • Appliances without refrigerant, such as washers, dryers and stoves.
  • Glass: old windows, mirrors and broken glass. Lay them flat and keep sharp pieces wrapped so nobody gets cut loading the rest.

Load heavy items first and spread them out, keep everything level with the top rim, and do not stack anything above it. A bin loaded over the rim cannot be hauled. When you are ready, book a roll-off dumpster online or call (801) 564-3164.

What cannot go in

  • Hazardous waste: chemicals, pesticides, herbicides, and anything radioactive.
  • Asbestos, including old flooring, siding or insulation you suspect contains it.
  • Wet paint, motor oil, fuel, antifreeze and other liquids.
  • Propane tanks and fuel containers.
  • Batteries, especially lithium-ion, and electronics such as TVs, monitors and computers.
  • Tires.
  • Appliances that contain refrigerant (Freon): refrigerators, freezers, window air conditioners.

These are on our terms page with the full list, and each one found in a load is ${{TRD:prohibited}} under those terms. Some of them are a fire or leak risk in a bin, and a load with them in it can be refused at the dump. The transfer station and landfill below take tires, fridges and electronics for a small fee.

Mattresses: call first

Mattresses and box springs need separate handling and carry an extra fee, so call (801) 564-3164 before you put one in the bin and we will tell you how to handle it. If you only have one or two, dropping them at the Weber County Transfer Station or Davis Landfill yourself is often simpler; prices are below.

Heavy material: call first

Concrete, dirt, brick, rock, sod and plaster are heavy, and they use up the weight allowance fast: {{TRD:tons15}} tons in a 15 yard, {{TRD:tons20}} tons in a 20 yard, {{TRD:tons25}} tons in a 25 yard. Weight over that is ${{TRD:over_ton}} per ton. Call us before you book a heavy load and we will tell you how much can safely go in.

Where the rest goes in Weber County

The Weber County Transfer Station is at 867 W Wilson Lane in Ogden. It is open Monday to Friday 7 a.m. to 5 p.m. and Saturday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., closed Sundays and major holidays. Waste Management has run its general waste and household hazardous waste collection since July 1, 2026, and the county suggests using 24th Street to Exchange Road because of train traffic on Wilson Lane. Weber County Solid Waste

Its rate sheet, as of July 1, 2026, lists these special items, each on top of the base disposal charge:

  • Tires: $4.00 each, up to four per person and eight per vehicle per day.
  • Mattresses and box springs: $15.00 each.
  • White goods (appliances): $15 per load, plus a $10.00 fee if the Freon has not been removed from a refrigerator.
  • TVs and computer monitors: $5.00 each.
  • Household hazardous waste: $20.00 per load.

The sheet notes those rates do not include taxes, an energy surcharge or other fees. Weber County rate sheet

Where the rest goes in Davis County

Davis Landfill is at 1997 E 3500 N in Layton, open Monday to Saturday, 7 a.m. to 6 p.m. in summer and 7 a.m. to 5 p.m. in winter. Davis Landfill

Wasatch Integrated's fee schedule, effective September 2, 2026, charges $4.00 per tire, $15.00 per refrigerator and $15.00 per mattress or box spring. Household hazardous waste and electronic waste are accepted at no charge for district residents with proof of residency, and are not accepted from out-of-district customers. Clean dirt and sod are accepted as clean fill. Wasatch Integrated fee schedule

Some cities also run free cleanup dumpsters for residents, with their own banned-items lists. See city dumpster days in Weber and Davis County.

Straight answers

Quick answers

Can you put appliances in a dumpster?

Washers, dryers and stoves, yes. Refrigerators, freezers and AC units that contain refrigerant, no: our terms prohibit them in the bin. The Weber County Transfer Station takes white goods for $15 a load (plus $10 if the Freon has not been removed), and Davis Landfill charges $15 per refrigerator.

Can you put dirt in a dumpster?

Call first. Dirt is heavy and fills the weight allowance with very little of the bin used, so tell us roughly how much you have and we will tell you how much can go in.

Can you put electronics in a dumpster?

No. TVs, monitors, computers and batteries cannot go in our bins. Davis Landfill takes electronic waste at no charge for district residents, and the Weber County Transfer Station charges $5 per TV or monitor plus its base fee.

Can you put mattresses or tires in a dumpster?

Tires, no: they are prohibited in our bins. Mattresses need separate handling and an extra fee, so call before one goes in. Both are $4 per tire and $15 per mattress at the Weber County Transfer Station and Davis Landfill.

Can you put paint in a dumpster?

Not wet paint, or any other liquid. Take leftover paint to a household hazardous waste drop-off, and ask us before you throw out any paint cans.

Can you put furniture and glass in a dumpster?

Yes. Couches, tables, dressers and other furniture are fine, and so are old windows, mirrors and broken glass; lay glass flat and wrap sharp pieces.
